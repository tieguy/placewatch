# Miami bot: a second account, and region profiles for the link ranking

Date: 2026-11-12. Status: design, awaiting approval.

Decision marks: **[operator]** means the operator decided it in the
brainstorm. **[default]** means the brainstorm proposed it and the operator
did not object; the builder or operator may revisit it.

## Intent

A friend of the operator wants a public bot account for Miami, the way
@SFedits / `top500.thebay.wiki` serves the San Francisco Bay Area: a feed of
edits to the region's most important Wikipedia articles. **[operator]**

Miami is a one-off for this friend, and it is also the **alpha test for
adding cities**. **[operator]** Success therefore means two things:

1. The friend's Bluesky account posts edits to important Miami-area articles,
   reliably, with the same safety behaviour as the SF account (hidden
   revisions get their posts deleted).
2. The project learns, from a real second city, what adding a city costs:
   which steps were manual, where it got stuck, and whether the link-based
   ranking holds up outside SF. That learning feeds the later scaling work
   (SFED-1, SFED-11, and `docs/design-plans/2026-08-16-topic-membership-sources.md`).

## Goals

- **Part 1:** run Miami as a second entry in `config.accounts` in the
  existing bot process and Toolforge tool (`san-francisco-edit-stream`).
  **[operator: route A]**
- **Part 2:** make the link-based importance ranking region-agnostic via
  region profiles, prove it still reproduces SF, then run it for Miami.
  **[operator: region profiles]**
- An **onboarding log** for Miami: every manual step taken to stand the city
  up, and every point of friction, written as it happens. **[default]**

## Non-goals

- The self-serve web flow ("WikiProject in, bot out"), OAuth (SFED-24),
  bring-your-own-auth delivery (SFED-20), accounts-as-subscriptions (SFED-1
  phase 4). Miami is configured by the operator.
- Mastodon and Discord for Miami. **Bluesky only for now.** **[operator]**
- Wikidata claim-change notices for Miami. They stay SF-only
  (`lib/wikidata-claim-watch.js:52-63` hard-codes the nine Bay Area counties).
  **[default]**
- Showing Miami on the web coverage page and `/api/topics.json`
  (`public/server.js:56-61` reads `accounts[0]` only). Record it in the
  onboarding log instead. **[default]**
- Ranking a platform topic from the database or from dumps on Toolforge (the
  full SFED-11). Part 2 stays a hand-run, laptop pipeline that publishes a
  JSON list. **[operator: chose region profiles over full SFED-11]**
- A separate Toolforge tool per city. **[operator: rejected in favour of A]**

## Anti-goals (would make this a failure even if Miami posts)

- Any change in what the SF account posts, or in its envvar names.
- A Miami post that cannot be deleted when its revision is hidden on-wiki.
- A topic subscriber (e.g. the live Venezuela feed) receiving a post twice
  because there are now two accounts.
- An SF-only edit reaching the Miami account, or the reverse.
- An SF ranking that silently changes because of the refactor.

## Constraints

- Pushes to `fork/integration` deploy within ~15 minutes (autoupdate job).
  Every Part 1 change must be safe to deploy before the Miami account exists.
- Wikimedia API etiquette: serial requests only, honour Retry-After, the
  anonymous ~200 req/min cap. `lib/mw-api.js` already does this; the
  ranking must keep using it.
- Invariants from SFED-1 that still hold: topic-only edits never reach account
  channels (the `resolveConsumers` gate at `page-watch.js:121-151` and its
  incident-regression test in `test/fan-out.test.js`); the revdel sweeper must
  be able to take down every post the bot made.
- The Bluesky handle and app password do not exist yet: the friend creates the
  account and gives the operator the app password. **[operator]** Launch waits
  on that; nothing else should.

## Approaches considered

**How to run Miami** (operator picked A):

- **A. Second account in the running bot.** Days to a couple of weeks. Exercises
  a second account end to end; shares the SF process (an OOM or crash, SFED-5,
  takes down both); the next city is still a config edit and a deploy.
- **B. Miami as a platform topic delivering to Bluesky.** The scaling end-state,
  but weeks of work: credential references, non-Discord subscription delivery,
  collapse/threading as delivery capabilities, WikiProject membership sources.
  The friend waits for all of it.
- Dropped: a separate Toolforge tool per city (copy-paste, teaches nothing
  about scaling, doubles ops); building the web flow first (months, still
  Discord-only).

**How to make the ranking generic** (operator picked 2):

1. Hand-edit the constants and rerun. Not generic; clobbers SF caches.
2. **Region profiles**: constants move to a per-region file, scripts take
   `--region`, caches per region, `rank.js` moves to `integration`.
3. Full SFED-11: rank topics from the DB using dumps on Toolforge. Unused until
   route B exists.

## Part 1: Miami as a second account

### What exists

`page-watch.js:648-650` already runs `inspect(account, edit)` for every
account, and `lib/watchlist-sync.js:249-268` refreshes each account's
`watchlist_source` on its own timer. Collapse windows and thread refs are keyed
per account (`page-watch.js:493-514`). Claim-watch skips accounts without a
`wikidata_claims` stanza (`lib/wikidata-claim-watch.js:436-437`,
`page-watch.js:519`). The Bluesky client has no shared session
(`lib/bluesky-client.js:34-45`).

### What must change

1. **Per-account secrets.** `lib/config.js:4-9` injects the Bluesky, Mastodon
   and Discord secrets into `accounts[0]` only, so Miami would log in with an
   undefined password. Proposal **[default]**: each account may carry an `id`
   (e.g. `"miami"`); its secrets come from `SFEDITS_<ID>_BLUESKY_PASSWORD`
   (and the Mastodon/Discord equivalents). `accounts[0]` keeps today's
   unprefixed names, so nothing changes for SF. Keep the existing rule that a
   set-but-unmatched secret is a startup error, and add the mirror case: an
   account whose delivery needs a secret that is missing should fail loudly at
   startup, not on every edit.

2. **Posted log records which account posted, and the sweeper deletes with the
   right credentials.** Today a log entry is `{host, revId, page,
   deliveries:[{type, postId, ...}]}` with no account identity
   (`lib/post-log.js:84-101`), and the sweeper runs for `accounts[0]` only
   (`page-watch.js:634-635`). A hidden Miami revision would be "deleted" with
   SF's Bluesky credentials, fail, and retry forever; the Miami post would stay
   up. Required: each recorded delivery carries the account (and, for
   config deliveries, the credential source); **one** sweeper resolves
   credentials per recorded delivery; entries without the field are treated as
   `accounts[0]` (legacy). This is substantially SFED-8 steps 1–3; lifting the
   `credential_ref` guard (`lib/delivery.js:191-201`, SFED-8 step 4) is
   optional here and the builder may fold it in or leave it. Watch
   `lib/post-log.js`'s `host:revId` keying (`entryKey`/`updateEntries`): with
   two accounts, one revision can have two entries, and the rewrite must not
   merge or duplicate them.

3. **Topic subscriptions fan out once per edit, not once per account.**
   `resolveConsumers` (`page-watch.js:153-175`) loads subscriptions for the
   edit's topics on every account's pass, and `sendStatus` delivers them
   (`page-watch.js:423-433`). With two accounts, every topic subscriber gets
   each post twice and the health/quarantine counters double. The fix must
   keep the account/topic isolation gate and its regression test intact; how
   to restructure is the builder's call.

4. **Miami account config** in `config.base.json` **[default unless noted]**:
   - `id: "miami"`.
   - `watchlist_source`: `project: "Miami"`, `importance: ["Top","High","Mid"]`
     (about 770 articles per WP 1.0 on 2026-11-11), `refresh_hours: 24`. When
     Part 2 lands, this switches to `titles_url` pointing at the Miami ranked
     list (one-line change).
   - `bluesky.identifier`: the friend's handle, once known. **[operator:
     Bluesky only; friend creates the account]**
   - `deliveries: [{ type: "bluesky", edit_filters: { bots: false, minor:
     false, substantive_only: true } }]`, same filters as SF.
   - `template` and `collapse` copied from SF.
   - No `discord`, `mastodon` or `wikidata_claims` stanzas.

   **Verify the PageAssessments name.** WP 1.0 lists the project as `Miami`;
   a wrong name returns zero pages, `lib/watchlist-sync.js:208-233` logs a
   failure, and the account silently posts nothing. Check by hand before
   launch (`list=projectpages&projects=Miami`, with `pasubprojects=true`),
   and confirm the bot's `✓ Watchlist sync` line shows a non-zero count after
   deploy.

   **Verify the count, not just non-zero.** The ~770 figure comes from WP 1.0's
   importance categories, which the `{{WikiProject Miami}}` banner fills
   directly. The bot's fetch (`lib/watchlist-sync.js:77-83`) does not pass
   `pasubprojects`. The launch check compares the bot's Top/High/Mid count
   against WP 1.0's within normal drift. If it is far short, the Miami pages
   are tagged through a subproject: add a per-account opt-in flag for
   `pasubprojects`, default off, so SF's fetch is unchanged. **[default]**

5. **Deploy order.** Code changes (1–3) ship first, with no Miami account in
   config, and must leave SF unchanged. The Miami account stanza and the
   `SFEDITS_MIAMI_BLUESKY_PASSWORD` envvar go in only once the friend has
   created the account.

### Tests required

- Config: two accounts, secrets land on the right account; missing Miami secret
  fails at startup; SF's unprefixed names still work.
- Fan-out: an edit matching a topic, with two accounts configured, delivers to
  each subscriber exactly once; an SF-only edit reaches only SF's deliveries;
  a Miami-only edit reaches only Miami's; an edit in both watchlists reaches
  both accounts. The existing Venezuela incident test stays green, unchanged.
- Sweeper: a hidden revision posted by account 2 is deleted with account 2's
  credentials; a legacy log entry with no account field is swept as account 0.
- No existing test uses two accounts today; these are new fixtures.

### Live verification

One real Miami-area edit produces exactly one Bluesky post on the Miami
account and nothing on SF channels; a topic subscriber's channel gets one post
per matching edit across a day.

## Part 2: region profiles for the link ranking

### What exists

The method is in `docs/importance-ranking-methodology.md`: a universe of
WikiProject-tagged pages ∪ Wikidata items in the region (is-here properties
P131/P159/P276/P39), ranked by canonical editorial inlinks (prose links,
citations and navboxes excluded, redirects folded), with a lead relevance gate
that drops articles whose lead does not mention a regional term. Against SF's
human ratings it correlates at r = 0.520.

SF-specific values are hard-coded: `PROJECT` (`scripts/reassess.js:55`),
`BAY_AREA_TERMS`/`BAY_AREA_RE` (`scripts/reassess.js:62-72`), `COUNTIES` and
`PROPERTIES` (`scripts/reassess-untagged.js:35-37`), the claim-target file
read at `scripts/reassess.js:522-528`, and the single cache directory
`data/reassess/`. `scripts/rank.js` exists only on
`fork/place-bot-platform-design` (`README-FOR-OTHER-CITIES.md:177-180`).
HTTP already goes through `lib/mw-api.js`.

### What to build

1. **Region profile files** **[default: location and format]**, e.g.
   `regions/sfba.json` and `regions/miami.json`, holding: WikiProject name,
   wiki, anchor QIDs, place terms, term exclusions, and anything else the
   scripts currently hard-code for SF.
2. **`--region <name>`** on `reassess.js`, `reassess-untagged.js` and
   `rank.js`, with caches under `data/reassess/<region>/`. The builder decides
   what happens to the existing SF caches (move them under `sfba/` so the
   reproduction check can use them).
3. **Bring `rank.js` onto `integration`** from `place-bot-platform-design`,
   adapted to the profile.
4. **Term exclusions.** The Miami gate must not let through "Miami University"
   (Ohio), Miami, Oklahoma, and similar. The profile carries exclusions; the
   builder chooses the mechanism.
5. **Miami profile** **[default]**: WikiProject `Miami`; anchors = the
   counties of the Miami metropolitan area (Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach;
   builder looks up the QIDs and checks the WikiProject's stated scope);
   en.wikipedia; a starting term list (Miami, Miami-Dade, Miami Beach, Fort
   Lauderdale, Broward, Palm Beach, Hialeah, Coral Gables, Everglades, …) that
   the builder refines from the run's report.

### Acceptance checks

- **SF reproduction:** running the SF profile on the existing SF caches yields
  the same ranking and the same `watchlist-500.json` as today. This is the
  proof the refactor changed nothing. "Same" means identical title order in
  `ranking.json`, `watchlist-500.json` and `watchlist-2500.json`; timestamps
  such as `generated_at` may differ. **[default]** The claims stage (which
  reads the bot's `data/wikidata-claim-targets.json` from Toolforge) is not
  needed for the ranking and may be skipped in the reproduction. **[default]**
  The SF caches (`data/reassess/`, gitignored) are not in this checkout; see
  "Open question" below.
- **Miami validation report:** the Pearson r of canonical inlinks against
  Miami's own human importance ratings (about 2,450 rated articles),
  reported next to SF's 0.520. Reported, not a gate. **[default]**
- **Miami top of list reviewed by hand** before publishing (the report's
  untagged high-rankers, as `README-FOR-OTHER-CITIES.md:192-195` warns).

### Publishing and the switch

Publish Miami's ranked list the way SF's is published (a JSON file served by
the tool's webservice). Then switch the Miami account's `watchlist_source` to
`titles_url`. The switch is the operator's call after reading the report.
**[default]**

## Order

**[default]** Part 1 and Part 2 are independent and can be planned and built
in parallel. Miami launches on the WikiProject's Top/High/Mid ratings as soon
as Part 1 is deployed and the friend's account exists; it switches to the
ranked list when Part 2 is done. The diff between the two lists goes into the
onboarding log as alpha data.

## Onboarding log

A markdown file, e.g. `docs/onboarding/2026-11-miami.md` **[default]**,
recording: each manual step (envvars, config, name checks, deploy), time
spent, what broke or confused, and the ratings-vs-ranking diff. Its purpose is
to become requirements for SFED-11 and the membership-sources web flow.

## Open question

Where are the SF ranking caches (`data/reassess/`) that produced the committed
`public/watchlist-500.json`? They are gitignored and absent from this checkout.
If they exist on another machine, the reproduction check uses them. If they
are gone, Part 2 starts with a snapshot step: run the **pre-refactor** code on
a fresh SF crawl (~18.5k articles), save the outputs, and require the
post-refactor code to match that snapshot on the same caches. That adds a full
SF crawl to Part 2, and the result will not match today's published file.
